Abstract
01

Mawjood is a centralized web-based lost and found platform designed to digitize property recovery workflows across public facilities. Traditional approaches — paper logs and verbal handoffs — are fragmented, error-prone, and offer no transparent tracking. Mawjood connects citizens who have lost belongings with location-specific offices that manage found inventories. The platform supports structured report submission, automated case matching, real-time status notifications, and multi-role administration, built on Java Spring Boot, Thymeleaf, and MySQL. It is applicable to airports, malls, universities, parks, and any public facility requiring organized lost property management.

02
Background
Introduction

Public facilities such as airports, malls, universities, and parks handle hundreds of lost-property incidents annually. Conventional approaches — paper ledgers, verbal handoffs, and informal social-media notices — are fragmented, unreliable, and leave users with no visibility into case progress.

The absence of a standardized process leads to unclaimed belongings, administrative overhead, and citizen frustration. A structured digital workflow is needed to unify the process under a single accessible platform for both organizations and the public.

Mawjood fills this gap: a role-aware web application where public users file structured reports and authorized office staff manage inventory, perform case matching, and issue official status updates through a controlled digital pipeline.

Project Goals
Objectives
  • 1
    Develop a centralized digital platform for managing lost and found cases across diverse public facilities.
  • 2
    Enable citizens to submit structured loss reports and receive transparent, real-time status updates.
  • 3
    Allow location offices to register found items and maintain a controlled, searchable digital inventory.
  • 4
    Implement an automated case-matching workflow linking user reports to office-held found items.
  • 5
    Provide role-based access control for administrators, office staff, and public users.
  • 6
    Reduce administrative overhead and improve the efficiency of the property recovery process.

Summary & Impact
Conclusions

Mawjood demonstrates that a well-structured, lightweight web platform can substantially improve lost-property management in public facilities. The system's modular architecture provides a scalable foundation, and its role-based design adapts to diverse organizational contexts without modification.

The project validates the effectiveness of the chosen stack — Spring Boot, Thymeleaf, and MySQL — for building robust institutional web applications with clean, maintainable code.

Future directions include a mobile application, AI-powered item description matching, and integration with facility management APIs.
